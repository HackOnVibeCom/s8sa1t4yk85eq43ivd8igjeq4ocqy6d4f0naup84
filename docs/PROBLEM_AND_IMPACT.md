# Problem and impact

## Problem

People with disabilities, caregivers and companions can find it difficult to plan visits when venue accessibility information is missing, vague, outdated or unsupported by evidence. A label such as "accessible" may not explain the practical details a person needs before travelling, such as entrance access, step-free routes, lifts, seating or accessible restrooms.

## Proposed solution

CANIGO is a community-oriented discovery prototype that brings venue information, accessibility attributes, photographs and community observations into one journey. People can browse information, contribute observations and use an AI assistant to navigate supported parts of the application.

The design emphasizes evidence and community context rather than treating an AI-generated answer as a certification. Reports and confirmations are signals for review, not proof that a venue meets a standard.

## Intended users

- People with mobility, sensory, cognitive or other access needs
- Caregivers, family members and travel companions
- Community members who want to share recent accessibility observations
- Venue operators and moderators who want to understand and respond to reported access barriers

These are intended audiences, not a claim of measured adoption or customer validation.

## Potential impact

If supported with accurate, maintained data and effective community moderation, CANIGO could help people make more informed visit decisions and make accessibility barriers more visible. The current repository is an early prototype with sample data and incomplete persistence in several user journeys. No real-world impact measurement, user study, partnership or outcome is claimed.

## Principles for responsible use

1. Make the evidence source and its age visible.
2. Do not describe a venue as universally accessible based on a single attribute or photograph.
3. Keep user observations distinguishable from verified facts.
4. Provide a path to correct or report inaccurate information.
5. Treat AI as an interface aid, not an independent accessibility auditor.
6. Avoid publishing personally identifying or sensitive information in public contributions.
